How the next step is picked

  • Substation campaign The end substations are located and the line is planned or further along. Always the choice for 765 kV, which is not tapped: projects connect at the substations.
  • Line campaign The route is set (an upgrade of an existing line, an approved route, or a line being built) and the line is 345 kV or below, so it can be tapped.
  • Follow updates Still proposed, or the end substations are not located yet.
  • Informational Serves one generator or one large load, so it adds no new shared capacity.

Sources: ERCOT's Transmission Project and Information Tracking report (TPIT, July 2026 update), the Line Watch project cards, and for locations the HIFLD national substation map, OpenStreetMap (data © OpenStreetMap contributors) and points researched from utility filings and maps (each pin says how it was placed). "About to enter construction" is estimated from an in-service date within 18 months unless a card says the route is approved, and a planned line whose in-service date has passed shows as "in construction" until ERCOT's next report confirms it. Landowners near the lines

Every week the landowner search takes the end substations on the watch list, finds every parcel within 2 miles, groups the parcels by owner, scores each owner and picks a next step: a letter, a salesperson call, or a Wendy call for owners who opted in. Owners show as type and initials here; the full list with names and mailing addresses goes to Jaiden as a private file.

It covers Texas today, using the free statewide parcel layer. It is built to go nationwide: other states switch on as free state parcel layers are added or once K2 picks a paid nationwide parcel data service.

Utility plans (IRPs)

Before a utility plans a new line, it plans how much power it will need. Its Integrated Resource Plan (IRP), filed with state regulators every 2 to 3 years, says how fast it expects demand to grow (data centers are now the biggest driver), how much new generation it needs, and what transmission it expects to build. Utilities with fast growth and big gaps are where new lines, and land demand, will come next.

Pilot: a first set of utilities in K2's priority markets, plus ERCOT's long-term forecast for Texas (most Texas utilities don't file IRPs). Every number links to its source; confidence is marked per section.

What this page is

K2 Renew buys land before the grid gets to it. Land near a new high-voltage line, and above all near the substations at each end, becomes worth much more once the line is built. This page lists the new lines that matter across the US, shows how far along each one is, says what K2 should do now, and lists the landowners near each line's end substations.

A line monitor checks the public sources every day at 7am and 5pm Central and adds anything new; the Texas lines list is checked for a new ERCOT report at the same times. The same items also go to the K2 Watch tab of the K2 Transmission Intel Plan sheet. Every Monday the landowner search reruns on every end substation, so new lines bring new landowners automatically.

Reading a project

Every new line goes through the same steps. The earlier K2 hears about it, the cheaper the land. Texas uses ERCOT and the Texas PUC; elsewhere the regional grid operator (MISO, SPP, PJM and others) plans the line and a state commission approves the route.

Importance is High for 345 kV and bigger lines in K2's priority markets, the big ones that open up the most land, and Medium for smaller lines or ones further from K2's focus (Northern Virginia is always Medium). Use the region menu to see one part of the country.

Why the end substations matter: the route can move during the case, but the two end substations almost never do. Land next to them is the safest early bet.

Using it day to day

  1. Check Latest signals for anything marked NEW. Those arrived since the watch started.
  2. Click a stage on the strip at the top, or a window (Open now, Act fast, Late), to narrow the list. The search box takes a line, county, utility or case number.
  3. Click a project to open it: where it runs, the end substations, case numbers, sources and what to do next.
  4. Press Ask about this line to start a question about that project in the assistant.
  5. Always open the source link before acting. The page is a guide, not the official record.

Where the information comes from

  • ERCOT's Transmission Project and Information Tracking report (TPIT): every planned ERCOT project with its end substations, voltage, status and in-service date. It feeds the Texas lines tab.
  • ERCOT Regional Planning Group meeting documents (stage 2).
  • Utility project pages: Oncor, AEP Texas, LCRA and CPS Energy (stage 3).
  • Texas PUC filings: new route cases, and new filings in cases already on the list (stages 4 to 6).
  • News about new lines and route fights across the US.

Items that don't matter to K2 are dropped before they reach this page: repairs of existing lines, lines under 138 kV, short hookups for single wind or solar farms, and routine case paperwork.

Not covered yet: most utilities outside Texas (only news covers them), county commissioners court agendas, and county easement records. ERCOT's planning email list is faster than the meeting pages, but it needs a personal subscription.
