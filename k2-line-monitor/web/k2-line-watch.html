Utility plans (IRPs)

Before a utility plans a new line, it plans how much power it will need. Its Integrated Resource Plan (IRP), filed with state regulators every 2 to 3 years, says how fast it expects demand to grow (data centers are now the biggest driver), how much new generation it needs, and what transmission it expects to build. Utilities with fast growth and big gaps are where new lines, and land demand, will come next.

Pilot: a first set of utilities in K2's priority markets, plus ERCOT's long-term forecast for Texas (most Texas utilities don't file IRPs). Every number links to its source; confidence is marked per section.

What this page is

K2 Renew buys land before the grid gets to it. Land near a new high-voltage line, and above all near the substations at each end, becomes worth much more once the line is built. This page lists the new lines that matter, shows how far along each one is, and says what K2 should do now.

A line monitor checks the public sources twice every weekday (7:45am and 12:45pm Central) and adds anything new. The same items also go to the K2 Watch tab of the K2 Transmission Intel Plan sheet.

Reading a project

Every new line goes through the same steps. The earlier K2 hears about it, the cheaper the land.

5Route case at the PUC. All route options and the landowner list are public.About 6 months to a decisionAct fast

7The utility is buying easements and building.Too late for most dealsLate

Importance is High for 345 kV and 765 kV lines, the big ones that open up the most land, and Medium for smaller lines or ones further from K2's focus.

Why the end substations matter: the route can move during the case, but the two end substations almost never do. Land next to them is the safest early bet.

Using it day to day

  1. Check Latest signals for anything marked NEW. Those arrived since the watch started.
  2. Click a stage on the strip at the top, or a window (Open now, Act fast, Late), to narrow the list. The search box takes a line, county, utility or case number.
  3. Click a project to open it: where it runs, the end substations, case numbers, sources and what to do next.
  4. Press Ask about this line to start a question about that project in the assistant.
  5. Always open the source link before acting. The page is a guide, not the official record.

Where the information comes from

  • ERCOT Regional Planning Group meeting documents (stage 2).
  • Utility project pages: Oncor, AEP Texas, LCRA and CPS Energy (stage 3).
  • Texas PUC filings: new route cases, and new filings in cases already on the list (stages 4 to 6).
  • News about new lines and route fights across the US.

Items that don't matter to K2 are dropped before they reach this page: repairs of existing lines, lines under 138 kV, short hookups for single wind or solar farms, and routine case paperwork.

Not covered yet: most utilities outside Texas (only news covers them), county commissioners court agendas, and county easement records. ERCOT's planning email list is faster than the meeting pages, but it needs a personal subscription.
